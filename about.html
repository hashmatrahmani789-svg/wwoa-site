About WWOA

From a service-driven idea to a mission still growing.

The story of how three Afghans, united by a shared belief, built an institution to serve their country.

Who We Are

An independent, non-political, non-profit institution

Wahaj Welfare Organization for Afghanistan (WWOA) is an independent, non-profit, non-political, and non-governmental organization established in 1393 (2014) to serve the people of Afghanistan and contribute to the country's sustainable development.

As a social and development-focused institution, WWOA concentrates its work on improving quality of life, empowering different segments of society, and supporting sustainable development. Its activities span education, technical and vocational training, health, public awareness, agriculture, empowerment of women and youth, social services, and humanitarian assistance.

WWOA is registered with Afghanistan's Ministry of Economy under registration number 3198.

Our Founding Story

From light and hope, a name — and a mission

2014 — A Shared Belief

Three committed members of the Afghan community — Engineer Imam Jan Wahaj Stanikzai, Haji Wahidullah Walizai, and Fazl Hadi — came together around a shared belief: that service to the people, community empowerment, and a better future for Afghanistan were worth building toward. Having witnessed the daily struggles people faced — limited access to education, healthcare, and vocational skills — they set out to build a bridge between community needs and development opportunity.

2014 — Founded in Jalalabad

WWOA was founded in Jalalabad, Nangarhar Province. From the very beginning, the organization treated service to the people not as a temporary activity, but as a human and social mission. The name "Wahaj" was inspired by light, hope, and awareness — values the founders hoped would be reflected in the lives of the people they served. Following official registration with the Ministry of Economy of Afghanistan, WWOA formally began its work.

Relocation to Kabul

As WWOA's activities expanded and the need for a broader national presence grew, the head office was relocated from Jalalabad to Kabul — opening greater opportunities for partnerships, skilled personnel, and larger-scale programs.

2025 — A New Chapter

Internal leadership and management responsibilities were handed over to Ms. Kereshma Safi. With this transition, while preserving WWOA's core goals, values, and mission, new efforts began to strengthen the organizational structure, recruit skilled professionals, improve program quality, and expand the scope of services.

Our Focus Areas

How we serve Afghan communities

Education & Training

Education, technical and vocational training, and skill-building programs for underserved communities.

Health & Hygiene

Free medical camps, primary healthcare awareness, and hygiene education for displaced and host communities.

Women & Youth Empowerment

Vocational training, psychosocial support, and confidence-building programs for women and young people.

Social Services

Public awareness, social mobilization, and community-based support services.

Agriculture

Programs supporting rural livelihoods and agricultural development.

Humanitarian Assistance

Direct support and relief for vulnerable and disadvantaged Afghans in need.
